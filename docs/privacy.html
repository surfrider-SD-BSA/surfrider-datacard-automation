Privacy Policy

Data Cards (Tally) for iOS · Effective 6 September 2026

This app collects no data about you, and sends nothing about your cleanup data to us or to anyone else. The reading happens on your device, offline. We run no server, and there is nowhere for your data to be sent.

There is one place the app talks to a third party, and only if your chapter has set it up: it can open a scan out of your chapter’s shared Google Drive folder, which means signing in to Google. That is described in full under Signing in to Google Drive below. A build made without a Google project configured has no Drive button and makes no network request of any kind.

The rest of this page explains what all of that means in practice, because claims this broad deserve detail.

What the app does with your cleanup data

Data Cards reads scanned beach cleanup data cards and turns them into a spreadsheet. The scans you open, the readings the app produces, the corrections you type, and the drafts it saves between sessions are all stored in the app's own private storage on your iPhone.

They are not uploaded, backed up to us, analyzed, or shared. There is no server of ours to send them to — the app has no account with us and no remote component we operate. The reading and recognition run entirely on your phone.

What we collect

Nothing. Specifically, the app does not collect, transmit, or store on our behalf:

  • Personal information of any kind
  • Usage data, analytics, crash telemetry, or diagnostics
  • Location data
  • Device identifiers or advertising identifiers
  • Contacts, photos, or any other library on your phone

There are no third-party analytics libraries, advertising SDKs, or tracking frameworks in the app. Nothing about your use of it is tracked, and no data is shared with any third party for any purpose.

Permissions

Files you choose. The app can open a PDF you pick yourself, or one you send to it from another app through the iOS share sheet. It only ever sees the file you deliberately hand it. It does not browse your files, and it takes a copy rather than opening the original in place, so it never has permission to modify your document.

A file shared into the app is placed in a private storage area shared only between Data Cards and its own share extension. The app moves it out and deletes the original the next time you open it, and anything left there for more than a week is discarded automatically.

Camera. This version of the app does not use the camera and will not ask for camera access. The app declares a camera usage description because photographing cards is a feature under development, disabled in this release.

Photo library. The app never requests access to your photos, and cannot read from or write to your photo library.

Signing in to Google Drive

Chapters keep their scans in a shared Google Drive folder. If your chapter has configured the app for that folder, the capture screen offers Choose from Drive, and pressing it signs you in to your own Google account so you can pick a scan without downloading it by hand first.

This is optional and off unless configured. A build made without a Google project has no such button and makes no network request at all. If you never press it, nothing in this section applies to you.

What the sign-in grants:

  • The app asks for exactly one scope, https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.file. That grants access to the individual files you pick in Google’s own file picker, and to nothing else in your Drive. The app cannot list your folders and cannot open a file you did not choose.
  • The broader drive.readonly scope is deliberately not used. It would mean asking a beach cleanup volunteer to grant a transcription tool the run of their entire Drive.

What moves, and where:

  • The scan is downloaded onto your phone from a folder that already holds it, and then read by the same code that reads a file you picked from Files. Nothing about a card is uploaded to Google.
  • If you choose to send a finished spreadsheet back, it is written to the same Drive folder the scan came from, and only when you ask for that.
  • Nothing goes to us at either step. We have no server, no copy of your file, and no way to see that any of this happened.

What Google learns: that your account opened that file, which it would have learned from you downloading the same file by hand.

The sign-in itself runs in a real browser view showing the address bar, so you can see you are typing your password into accounts.google.com and not into this app. The app never sees your Google password. The resulting access token is held in memory for as long as the app is running, is written to no file and to no Keychain, and is gone when you quit. No refresh token is requested, so the grant expires on its own in about an hour and cannot be renewed silently.

Google’s file picker is a web component with no native version, so it is loaded from this project’s public GitHub Pages site. It receives an access token and returns a file identifier; it downloads nothing and never sees a card. Your use of Google Drive is also governed by Google’s own privacy policy.

To revoke the app’s access at any time, visit your Google account’s third-party access page and remove it.

When you share a spreadsheet

When you export your finished spreadsheet, the app writes the file and hands it to the standard iOS share sheet. From there, you decide where it goes — Files, AirDrop, Mail, or another app you have installed.

Anything that happens after you choose a destination is governed by that service's own privacy policy, not this one. The app has no say in it and no knowledge of it.

Children

The app is a tool for recording beach cleanup survey data. It is not directed at children, and since it collects no data at all, it collects none from children.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of the app changes what it does with data — for example, if the camera feature is released — this page will be updated before that version ships, and the effective date above will change.
